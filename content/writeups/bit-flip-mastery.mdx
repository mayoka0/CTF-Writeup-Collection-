# Bit-Flip Mastery

## Initial Reconnaissance

<CyberInfo type="exploit" title="Key Exploit">
  AES-CBC bit-flipping attack on the session cookie to change the 'role=user' to 'role=admin'.
</CyberInfo>

## Enumeration

- **Encrypted Cookie:** Noticed the cookie was 32 bytes long, indicating two blocks of AES.
- **IV Discovery:** The first 16 bytes of the cookie were identified as the Initialization Vector.

## Exploitation

### Step 1: Target Identification
Identifying exactly which bit in the IV corresponds to the 'u' in 'user' within the first block of plaintext.

### Step 2: The Flip
Crafting a new IV by XORing the original IV bit with the XOR sum of the current plaintext ('u') and the target ('a').

<CyberInfo type="lesson" title="Lesson Learned">
  CBC mode without an HMAC or integrity check is vulnerable to structural manipulation of the plaintext.
</CyberInfo>

The AES-Walker machine provided a great challenge in practical cryptographic attacks.
